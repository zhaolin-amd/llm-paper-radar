#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: at 4-bit quantization the model is 19.5 GB, and sparsity reduces compute but not the bytes that must be loaded. Naive SSD offloading fails because layer N+1's expert selection depends on layer N's output, preventing early reads that could hide I/O latency behind compute. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a lightweight per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead—so SSD reads are pipelined with computation and the predicted expert set equals the routed set without dropping any tokens. An unmerged recovery LoRA, trained on the student path, compensates for both INT4 quantization loss and routing replacement. On a single 24 GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3 GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few benchmark points of its fp16 teacher across five public evaluations.

- 🎯 Prerouter head predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead, enabling SSD reads to overlap with compute and eliminating offload latency
- 💡 Recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates jointly for INT4 quantization loss and routing-prediction substitution errors
- 📊 35B MoE runs at 20 tok/s on a single 24 GB GPU with only 3 GiB peak active memory, within a few points of fp16 teacher on five benchmarks
- 🔧 Framework, checkpoints, and adapters are fully open-sourced; an 8B tier is also supported on the same framework

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Pruning / distillation** · topic 5/5 · practicality 5/5 · composite 10/10 · This paper targets the routed-expert weight loading bottleneck in MoE decoding, proposing a calibration-free channel selection method (top-k selection) and a reader built from model weights (ternary sketch + low-rank recovery). #### Summary
MoE models are bottlenecked during interactive decoding by loading routed-expert weights from memory at small batch sizes. Existing threshold-based channel selectors calibrated on offline data neither reliably control read budgets (a threshold targeting 18% reads only 12.9% on average) nor yield consistent accuracy gains. Top-k channel selection requires no calibration data and matches or beats threshold selectors in 40 of 43 comparisons at 13% and 18% budgets, while also eliminating the repetition-loop collapse that calibrated thresholds cause on Gemma 4. The authors further introduce a data-free reader built entirely from model weights—using a ternary sketch to screen channels and a low-rank map to recover skipped ones—achieving 98.1% of full-model accuracy at only 17.8% of routed-expert reads across three post-trained MoE models, with Triton kernels running 1.60–1.90× faster than optimized vLLM at batch size one and up to 2.69× at larger batches.

- 🎯 Top-k channel selection replaces calibrated thresholds, requiring zero calibration data and winning 40 of 43 accuracy comparisons at 13% and 18% read budgets
- 📊 Retains 98.1% full-model accuracy at only 17.8% of routed-expert reads across three post-trained MoE models
- 🔧 Triton kernels achieve 1.60–1.90× speedup over optimized vLLM at batch=1, up to 2.69× at larger batches
- 💡 Data-free reader uses a ternary sketch for channel screening plus a low-rank map for recovery, entirely derived from model weights
