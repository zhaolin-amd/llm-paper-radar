#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory rather than compute: a 35B-class model occupies 19.5GB at 4-bit, and expert sparsity reduces FLOPs per token without reducing bytes that must be fetched. Naive SSD offloading fails because layer N+1's expert IDs are unknown until layer N finishes, leaving no time to prefetch. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead—so expert reads from SSD can be issued early enough to fully overlap with compute, and a trained unmerged recovery LoRA compensates quality loss from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Prerouter predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead, enabling async SSD-to-GPU expert prefetch to hide offload latency
- 📊 35B MoE served at 20tok/s on a single 24GB machine with only 3GiB peak active memory, near fp16 teacher on five benchmarks
- 💡 Unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates quality loss from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement jointly
- 🔧 Framework covers 35B and 8B MoE tiers; code, checkpoints, and adapters are fully open-sourced

#### Summary
MoE models are bottlenecked by routed-expert weight loading during interactive decoding at small batch sizes. Existing channel selectors rely on offline-calibrated thresholds, but calibrated thresholds yield unstable read budgets (an 18%-target threshold actually reads only 12.9% on average) and cause repetition-loop collapse on Gemma 4. This work shows that top-k channel selection requires no calibration data and matches or beats the threshold variant in 40 of 43 comparisons at 13% and 18% budgets. Additionally, a calibration-free reader built entirely from model weights—using a ternary sketch to screen channels and a low-rank map to recover skipped ones—retains 98.1% of full-model accuracy at 17.8% routed-expert reads, with Triton kernels running 1.60–1.90x faster than optimized vLLM at batch one and up to 2.69x at larger batches.

- 🎯 Top-k channel selection eliminates the need for offline calibration data while fixing the per-token read budget

- 📊 Ternary sketch + low-rank map reader retains 98.1% accuracy at 17.8% reads; Triton kernels achieve 1.60–1.90x (batch 1) and up to 2.69x speedup over optimized vLLM
- 💡 Calibrated thresholds neither guarantee a fixed read budget nor consistently improve accuracy, and trigger repetition-loop collapse on Gemma 4
