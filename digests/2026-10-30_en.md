#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is constrained by weight memory—a 35B model requires 19.5GB at 4-bit—and naive SSD offloading fails because the next layer's expert selection cannot be known until the current layer's output is ready, preventing early prefetching. Edge0 introduces a prerouter: a per-layer head predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead, and its prediction is used directly as the routing decision, allowing SSD reads to be fully overlapped with compute. An unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student inference path compensates for quality loss from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, remaining within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Prerouter predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead and uses the prediction as the actual route, hiding SSD read latency behind compute
