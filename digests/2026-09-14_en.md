#### Summary
Agentic serving workloads consume orders of magnitude more tokens than chatbot workloads, stressing KV-cache capacity and decode-time bandwidth. Existing KV-eviction methods score cached keys against recent tokens, but agentic generation violates this assumption: think, act, tool, and other phases occupy measurably different query subspaces, causing recency-based scoring to systematically undervalue keys needed by upcoming phases. AgentKV maintains a small query buffer per phase and scores cached keys against the union of all phase buffers; it is further integrated into SGLang's persistent multi-turn serving path, carrying compressed KV state across turns and compacting retained KV pages online. #### Summary

**Survey / methodology & comparison** · topic 3/5 · practicality 3/5 · composite 6/10 · This paper is a survey-style empirical study that systematically tests the transferability of PTQ heuristics (protect the embedding table, allocate bits by module sensitivity, ranking-aware objective) on retrieval embedding models. The largest tested model is only 0.6B, and the target is retrieval embedders rather than generative LLMs. While the conclusions are useful reference for embedder quantization engineers, overall relevance is limited due to small model scale (<1B), non-generative task focus, and non-standard LLM evaluation benchmarks. On the practicality side, actionable quantization recommendations are provided (e.g., INT3 + distillation outperforms extreme PTQ), but the conclusions are task-specific and cannot be directly transferred to large-scale LLM deployment scenarios.

#### Summary
Weight-only post-training quantization heuristics inherited from LLM compression — protect the embedding table, allocate bits by module sensitivity, prefer ranking-aware objectives — are systematically tested on retrieval embedders across four architecture families, five checkpoints, and a grid of bit widths and group sizes. All three heuristics fail to transfer: the embedding table is never the dominant isolated protection priority, module sensitivity ordering becomes family-dependent at INT3 and breaks down entirely at INT2 where reconstruction error is comparable yet retention ranges from 1.3 to 65.9 percent of full precision, and the reconstruction proxy is unreliable for tensor-level protection decisions. A 109M distilled student at INT3 achieves 78.04 NDCG@10 in 68.4 MB, outperforming its 0.6B teacher under extreme PTQ (297.9 MB, 64.46 NDCG@10) on both size and quality, but only within the distillation task.

- 📊 109M INT3 distilled student (68.4 MB) reaches 78.04 NDCG@10, beating the 0.6B teacher's extreme PTQ (297.9 MB, 64.46 NDCG@10)
- 💡 At INT2, comparable reconstruction error yields retention ranging from 1.3% to 65.9% of full precision, exposing complete proxy metric failure
- ⚠️ Distillation gains are task-specific and findings do not directly transfer to large-scale generative LLM deployment
