#### Summary
Efficient edge inference for ternary LLMs (e.g., BitNet b1.58) has remained underexplored despite growing interest. Bitnet.cpp addresses this by building an inference system centered on a novel mpGEMM library that enables sub-2-bits-per-weight computation via two kernel designs: Ternary Lookup Table (TL), which overcomes spatial inefficiencies of prior bit-wise methods, and Int2 with Scale (I2_S), which guarantees lossless inference. The system achieves up to 6.25x speedup over full-precision baselines and up to 2.32x over low-bit baselines, and is publicly released for deployment on edge hardware including GPUs and ARM CPUs.

#### Summary
Existing structured pruning methods rely on greedy heuristics that produce myopic decisions and frequently miss target compression budgets by up to 33%. SNIPER is a two-stage framework that first solves a binary knapsack optimization over coarse-grained components (layers, attention heads, FFN dimensions) to obtain conditionally optimal parameter allocations given fixed importance estimates, then applies fine-grained pruning to enforce strict budget constraints. A new metric, CRAFT (Compression Ratio Adherence Factor), quantifies budget fidelity; SNIPER achieves a CRAFT score of 0.98. Evaluated across four architectures on 18 tasks spanning five domains against six state-of-the-art pruners, SNIPER achieves a mean rank of 1.25, demonstrating robust cross-architectural generalizability.
