#### Summary
Adaptive rounding methods like GPTQ round a real matrix to integers under a quadratic metric by processing entries sequentially and propagating rounding errors via a triangular feedback matrix—this is the one-sided case. The two-sided variant places nonsingular basis matrices on both the left and right of the residual; vectorizing converts the objective into a quadratic metric with a Kronecker-product Gram matrix, making the naive one-dimensional algorithm applicable but at quartic time complexity. GPTQ-2D achieves the same rounded result in cubic time by processing entries anti-diagonal by anti-diagonal, where entries on the same anti-diagonal are mutually independent and can be rounded in parallel.

- 🎯 Extends one-sided adaptive rounding (GPTQ) to a two-sided setting with left and right basis matrices
- 💡 Processes matrix entries anti-diagonal by anti-diagonal; entries on each anti-diagonal are independent and parallelizable
- 📊 Reduces time complexity from quartic to cubic in matrix dimension while producing identical rounded output
- ⚠️ Purely theoretical/algorithmic contribution; no LLM benchmark experiments reported in the abstract

#### Summary
KV cache size grows linearly with context length, rapidly exhausting GPU memory during LLM inference. The proposed counter-causal eviction scheme scores cached tokens by applying a counter-causal attention mask—where each position attends only to its future context—so that tokens well-predicted by more recent tokens are deemed redundant and evicted. Scoring reuses existing key-value representations already in the cache, is in-distribution, and requires no additional training. A fast single-layer approximation restricts the counter-causal pass to the last transformer layer, achieving significant speedup per refresh cycle at marginal accuracy cost, with competitive or improved results over state-of-the-art methods across multiple open-source LLMs and benchmarks.

- 🎯 Counter-causal attention mask scores cached tokens by how well they are predicted by future context; redundant entries are evicted
- 💡 Scoring fully reuses existing KV cache contents—in-distribution, no extra training or calibration data needed
- 🔧 Single-layer approximation limits counter-causal pass to the last transformer layer, significantly cutting per-refresh-cycle cost at marginal accuracy loss
- 📊 Achieves competitive or improved generation quality versus state-of-the-art KV eviction methods across multiple open-source LLMs and benchmarks

#### Summary
FFN layers dominate memory traffic and computation in LLM inference, and existing training-free activation sparsification methods suffer significant quality degradation at high sparsity due to poor channel-selection strategies. Prox identifies that the SwiGLU intermediate state is a highly informative channel-selection signal, but obtaining it exactly is computationally expensive. The two-stage Prox framework addresses this by using input sparsity and quantized proxy weights to construct a shared channel mask in Stage 1, then exactly computing only the selected channels in Stage 2, enabling sparse execution of all three projections. Across ten LLMs from six model families, Prox outperforms all training-free baselines at every sparsity level, achieves up to 1.99× end-to-end decoding speedup at 70% FFN sparsity, and is compatible with quantization and sparse attention.

- 🎯 Two-stage training-free framework: Stage 1 builds a channel mask via sparse inputs and quantized proxy weights; Stage 2 executes exact sparse computation
- 📊 Up to 1.99× end-to-end decoding speedup at 70% FFN sparsity, validated on 10 LLMs across 6 model families
- 💡 Key insight: sparse execution needs only the magnitude ranking of the intermediate state, not its exact values, avoiding costly dense forward passes
- 🔧 Compatible with quantization and sparse attention; fully training-free with no calibration data required
