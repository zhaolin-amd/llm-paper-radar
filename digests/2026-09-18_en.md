#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: a 35B model reaches 19.5GB at int4, and naive SSD offloading cannot hide read latency because expert selection for layer N+1 requires layer N's output. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead—enabling fully pipelined SSD prefetch with no dropped experts. A recovery LoRA trained on the student path (int4 + prerouter) recovers quality lost to both quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

#### Summary
W4A4 weight-activation quantization is hampered by activation outliers that reduce effective quantization resolution, and the relationship among existing mitigations—weight optimization, channel-wise scaling, and orthogonal rotation—has remained opaque. This work introduces an exact decomposition of the local quantization error into an activation-guided weight compensation term and an orthogonal residual, with the residual upper-bounded using persistent channel outlier and regular activation statistics. From this framework, practical design guidelines are derived for randomized Hadamard rotation, random sign selection, and channel scaling, explaining phenomena such as sign-based suppression of constructive interference among outlier channels and the recovery of SmoothQuant-style L∞ scaling via relaxed second-moment balancing. Backpropagation-free configurations evaluated on eight Llama and Mistral models achieve performance competitive with gradient-trained SpinQuant.
