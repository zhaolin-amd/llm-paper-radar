#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Trending / high-heat, no bucket** · topic 3/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 7/10 · This paper proposes reducing the draft model's hidden width to 1/2 or 1/4 of the target model's in speculative decoding, sitting at the intersection of draft model compression and inference acceleration. The main contribution is demonstrating end-to-end speedups (1.06–1.44x) for DFlash and EAGLE-3 drafter families on SGLang/B200/GB10 via width-narrowing without modifying the target model. The method is simple with a clear deployment path (validated on SGLang with NVFP4/MXFP4 quantization formats), and algorithmically constitutes a variant of hidden-state compression/distillation for drafters. However, evaluation lacks standard downstream benchmarks (MMLU/GSM8K, etc.), reporting only acceptance rate and wall-clock speedup, and the primary contribution leans toward inference-engine optimization rather than a new compression algorithm. By bucket classification, this work falls under compression-adjacent decoding acceleration, best suited for the trending bucket.

#### Summary
In speculative decoding, draft models that read the target's hidden states conventionally inherit the target's full hidden width. This paper demonstrates that the draft's entire residual stream can run at half or a quarter of that width while largely preserving token acceptance rates. For DFlash, the trained narrow output adapter folds into the vocabulary projection, leaving logits unchanged up to floating-point rounding. Evaluated on DFlash and EAGLE-3 drafters with Qwen3-8B (NVFP4) and GPT-OSS-20B (MXFP4) targets in SGLang, width-narrowing achieves 1.06–1.12x end-to-end speedup on a B200 and 1.13–1.44x on a GB10 (DGX Spark), with no narrow draft ever slower than full-width across all tested batch sizes.

- 🎯 Draft hidden width reduced to 1/2 or 1/4 of the target's width; target model unchanged
- 💡 DFlash narrow adapter folds into vocabulary projection, logit error bounded by floating-point rounding only
- 📊 End-to-end speedup 1.06–1.12x on B200, 1.13–1.44x on GB10; up to 1.21x at batch ≥32 on both GPUs
- 🔧 Validated in SGLang with NVFP4/MXFP4 quantization; no negative speedup observed at any batch size
