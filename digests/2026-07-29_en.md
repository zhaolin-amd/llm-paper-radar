#### Summary
End-to-end FP4 RL post-training suffers primarily from rollout activation quantization, where outliers stretch dynamic range and cause massive underflow to zero — not from training-side quantization error. Counterintuitively, restoring training to higher precision while keeping FP4 rollout worsens accuracy, identifying rollout-training mismatch as the principal failure mode. The paper introduces HiFloat4 (HiF4), a three-level hierarchical scaling format that preserves resolution under 4-bit budget, and Rollout-ResQ, a lightweight sparse residual correction applied only to FP4 rollout matmuls. On Qwen2.5-3B and Qwen2.5-Math-7B, HiF4 + Rollout-ResQ narrows the accuracy gap to BF16 from 4.9% to 1.1%; the same recipe applied to MXFP4 reduces the gap from 13.6% to 5.3%.

- 🎯 Rollout-ResQ adds a single sparse residual correction to FP4 rollout matmuls, recovering precision lost to outlier-driven underflow without increasing compute footprint
- 💡 HiF4's three-level hierarchical scaling preserves resolution within the tight 4-bit budget, serving as the enabling format for FP4 RL
- 📊 HiF4 + Rollout-ResQ closes the BF16 accuracy gap from 4.9% to 1.1%; on MXFP4, gap narrows from 13.6% to 5.3%
- ⚠️ HiF4 hardware support is not yet widespread; FP4 format choice is shown to be a key ceiling on recoverable accuracy
