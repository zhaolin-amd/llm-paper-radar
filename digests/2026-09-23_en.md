#### 🧭 Why this paper
**PTQ (post-training quantization)** · topic 5/5 · practicality 5/5 · composite 10/10 · This paper proposes a 'disaggregated quantization' (DQ) scheme that applies different quantization formats to the prefill and decode phases: NVFP4 low-precision activation quantization for prefill to accelerate prompt processing, and weight-only quantization (2–3 bit) for decode to reduce memory bandwidth pressure. It demonstrates significant accuracy gains on MMLU-Pro and MMMU-Pro, achieves a 1.78x TTFT speedup in llama.cpp, and is validated with vLLM integration. Models up to 2.8T parameters are tested; the approach is conceptually clean (separating checkpoints on top of existing GGUF/NVFP4 formats), calibration cost is reasonable (leveraging released decoder checkpoints), and deployment feasibility is strong—making it a high-quality PTQ work balancing engineering practicality and academic novelty.

#### Summary
Prefill and decode phases have fundamentally different quantization requirements: prefill benefits from low-precision arithmetic for prompt processing, while decode benefits from compact weights to reduce memory traffic. Disaggregated quantization (DQ) specializes computation formats, weights, and storage placement independently for each phase—removing activation quantization on decode to improve accuracy, and training separate compute-native prefill weights to accelerate prompt processing. Using released Qwen3.8-27B GGUF decoders, training an NVFP4 prefiller improves 1-bit accuracy by 32.5 points on MMLU-Pro and 35.3 points on MMMU-Pro without modifying the decode checkpoint. Offloaded disaggregated prefill (ODP) streams prefill weights from SSD, achieving a 1.78x time-to-first-token speedup over a weight-only baseline at 8K prompt length in llama.cpp, with accuracy validated in vLLM and models evaluated up to 2.8T parameters.

#### Summary
Serving 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware with SSD offloading is bottlenecked by the inability to prefetch layer N+1 experts before layer N's output is available, preventing I/O from hiding behind compute. Edge0 addresses this with a prerouter: a per-layer head predicts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead, and the prediction is used directly as the routing decision, enabling staged prefetch to match the actual routed set. A recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates for quality loss from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement, achieving 20tok/s on a single 24GB machine with 3GiB peak active memory and within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

#### Summary
KV-cache eviction is conventionally assessed via average quality-memory trade-offs, which can mask severe per-request degradation. This work reformulates eviction as a deployment risk-control problem: a material degradation event occurs when eviction drops a request's task utility beyond a tolerance relative to full-KV inference, and deployment risk is the population frequency of such events.
