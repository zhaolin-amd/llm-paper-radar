#### 🧭 Why this paper

The core idea is to use forward KL between the teacher and a pruned probe to identify disrupted predictions, then reweight calibration statistics to guide existing pruning criteria. #### Summary
Structured pruning reduces deployment cost of reasoning language models but degrades capabilities in ways that limit subsequent on-policy distillation (OPD) recovery, since OPD depends on student-generated trajectories. RECAL (Recovery-Aware Calibration) addresses this by adjusting calibration statistics before pruning: it computes forward KL between an unpruned teacher and a pruned probe to identify teacher-supported predictions disrupted by pruning, then reweights calibration statistics to steer existing pruning criteria toward preserving those predictions. The method is plug-and-play and requires no modification to the underlying pruning algorithm. Across multiple models and pruning methods, RECAL consistently improves post-OPD mathematical reasoning and code generation, with gains of up to 16.7 percentage points on AIME.

- 🎯 Forward KL between teacher and pruned probe identifies disrupted predictions; calibration statistics are reweighted to guide pruning criteria accordingly
- 📊 Up to 16.7 percentage-point gain on AIME; improvements in most code-generation comparisons after OPD recovery
- 💡 Plug-and-play design: compatible with existing pruning criteria without algorithmic modification
- 🔧 Analysis shows reduced residual damage at heavily affected tokens and persistent performance advantages throughout recovery

### 11. Deflating the Hessian: Rank-4 W4A4 Quantization for Multimodal Diffusion Transformers (8/10)

🔗 [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11315) · [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2610.11315)
📡 Sources: arxiv
🧪 diffusion · W4A4 PTQ + low-rank compensation · FLUX.1-dev · cal: Optimization-based PTQ with lower calibration cost than SVDQuant rank-32. · perf: Quantization speed up to 6.25× faster; rank requirement only 1/8 of the baseline.

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Diffusion** · topic 4/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 8/10 · This paper proposes a unified low-rank-assisted W4A4 PTQ framework for diffusion transformers, jointly optimizing a deflated Hessian and activation-noise surrogate to surpass rank-32 SVDQuant's PSNR/LPIPS at rank-4, with MMLU gains validated on Qwen3-8B (61.5%→68.2%). Evaluation spans 5 diffusion backbones (SANA-1.6B, FLUX.1-schnell, FLUX.1-dev, etc.), satisfying the `diffusion` quantization bucket. The algorithm is relatively lean (rank-4 vs. rank-32 significantly reduces compute, 6.25× faster quantization) and includes LLM validation, making it practically relevant. End-to-end inference latency data are not provided, slightly limiting practicality scores.

#### Summary
Existing low-rank PTQ methods for diffusion transformers either optimize the low-rank branch and residual quantization separately—requiring high ranks—or ignore activation quantization error in second-order weight updates. This work formulates low-rank-assisted W4A4 PTQ as a coupled calibration problem: eliminating the output-side low-rank factor yields a deflated Hessian that discounts residual errors already captured by the low-rank component, while an activation-noise surrogate suppresses activation quantization error. Across five diffusion backbones, rank-4 consistently outperforms rank-4 SVDQuant in PSNR and LPIPS, and further surpasses rank-32 SVDQuant on SANA-1.6B, FLUX.1-schnell, and FLUX.1-dev with an 8× smaller rank and up to 6.25× faster quantization. On Qwen3-8B, MMLU accuracy improves from 61.50% to 68.17% over rank-32 SVDQuant.

- 🎯 Unified W4A4 PTQ framework using a deflated Hessian and activation-noise surrogate for joint low-rank and residual optimization.
- 📊 Rank-4 outperforms rank-32 SVDQuant on SANA-1.6B, FLUX.1-schnell, and FLUX.1-dev with 8× smaller rank and up to 6.25× faster quantization.
- 📊 MMLU on Qwen3-8B improves from 61.50% to 68.17% over rank-32 SVDQuant at rank-4.
- 💡 Deflated Hessian avoids double-penalizing errors already absorbed by the low-rank branch, a key departure from prior methods.

#### 📎 Related methods / baselines
- SVDQuant — primary baseline; rank-4 proposed method surpasses its rank-32 on multiple metrics
