**PTQ (post-training quantization)** · topic 5/5 · practicality 5/5 · composite 10/10 · This work deeply investigates the optimization imbalance caused by MSE reconstruction loss in learning-based PTQ, finding that loss magnitudes vary dramatically across quantization stages, leading to severely uneven gradients and parameter updates. #### Summary
Learning-based PTQ methods commonly use MSE as the reconstruction loss, but the impact of varying loss magnitudes across sequential quantization stages has been overlooked. This work systematically studies how optimization evolves across stages and uncovers Optimization Imbalance: reconstruction loss magnitudes vary dramatically across stages, causing highly uneven gradient magnitudes and parameter updates under MSE. MSE directly translates this reconstruction loss scale into uneven optimization strength, degrading quantization quality in affected stages. RMSE variants defined at the sample, channel, token, and element levels are shown—both theoretically and empirically—to naturally decouple optimization strength from loss scale via implicit gradient normalization, serving as a drop-in MSE replacement that significantly improves representative learning-based PTQ methods across multiple LLM families, quantization settings, and benchmarks including MMLU, GSM8K, and HumanEval.

#### 🌊 Paper River

#### Summary
Running 35B MoE models on consumer hardware is blocked by two issues: weight memory (19.5GB at 4-bit) and SSD offloading latency, since the next layer's expert selection cannot begin until the current layer's output is ready. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead—so SSD reads can be fully overlapped with compute, and the predicted expert set is used directly as the routing decision without any dropping. An unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates quality lost to INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB GPU, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks, with framework, checkpoints, and adapters released as open source.

- 🎯 Prerouter predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead, fully overlapping SSD prefetch with compute; staged expert set equals the routed set
- 📊 35B MoE served at 20 tok/s on a single 24GB GPU with only 3GiB peak active memory, within a few points of fp16 teacher on five benchmarks
- 💡 Unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path jointly compensates INT4 quantization and routing-replacement quality loss
- 🔧 Framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open source; 8B tier supported on the same framework

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Pruning / distillation** · topic 3/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 7/10 · QK-Wanda is a direct extension of Wanda, coupling the pruning budget of query and key weights via closed-form scoring for gradient-free unstructured pruning with minimal algorithmic complexity and very low calibration cost. #### Summary
Wanda prunes LLM weights independently within each linear projection, ignoring the dot-product interaction between queries and keys. QK-Wanda scores query and key weights by their individual deletion costs under a pre-RoPE reconstruction objective, augmenting each projection's scores with information from the opposite projection and sharing a joint pruning budget. Scores are closed-form, requiring no gradients or weight updates, with full pruning taking only 1.3% longer than Wanda on A100. Evaluated on 15 models (0.5B–72B) across TinyLlama, Llama 2/3, and Qwen2.5, QK-Wanda reduces QK reconstruction error by 60% at 50% sparsity and 45% at 80%; on Llama 2 70B at 80% sparsity, WikiText-2 and C4 perplexity drop by 20.3% and 13.5% while zero-shot accuracy rises by 5.94 pp, though Llama-3.1-70B shows higher perplexity despite lower reconstruction error.

- 🎯 Jointly scores query/key weights using opposite-projection information via closed-form deletion costs, no gradients needed
- 📊 Average QK reconstruction error reduced by 60% at 50% sparsity and 45% at 80% sparsity vs. Wanda
- 📊 Llama 2 70B at 80% sparsity: WikiText-2 PPL −20.3%, C4 PPL −13.5%, zero-shot accuracy +5.94 pp
- ⚠️ Llama-3.1-70B shows higher PPL despite lower reconstruction error, exposing limits of local reconstruction as a quality proxy
