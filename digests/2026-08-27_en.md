#### Summary
Ultra-low-bit quantization (MXFP4, HiF4) of Multimodal LLMs causes substantial performance degradation, and ablations identify activation quantization—not weight quantization—as the dominant source of loss. To address this, Residual Fallback Quantization (RFQ) augments the primary ultra-low-bit activation path with an auxiliary quantized residual pathway that explicitly models and compensates for quantization errors, requiring no architectural changes and negligible computational overhead. Experiments on the video generation model Wan2.2 and the multimodal reasoning model Qwen3-VL show that RFQ consistently recovers a substantial portion of performance lost under MXFP4 and HiF4, significantly narrowing the gap to BF16 baselines across both generation and 4 reasoning benchmarks.

#### Summary
Pruning LLMs can amplify text degeneration—particularly repetition loops—even when perplexity and task accuracy remain largely intact. This work models decoding as a dynamical process and decomposes degeneration into loop entry risk and loop persistence, showing that persistence is governed by the escape mass assigned to plausible alternative tokens in the sampling set. Two post-pruning fine-tuning objectives are proposed: FOCUS reweights distillation toward high-confidence teacher regions to suppress probability leakage, while RePAIR employs onset-centered positive/negative continuation pairs with a margin loss to promote alternatives and block early commitment to loops. Both methods consistently reduce repetition and improve generation quality on open-ended continuation and instruction-based generation benchmarks.

- 🎯 Decoding degeneration decomposed into loop entry risk and loop persistence; persistence controlled by escape mass of plausible alternatives
- 💡 FOCUS reweights distillation to high-confidence teacher regions; RePAIR uses onset-centered pos/neg pairs with margin loss
- 📊 Both methods consistently reduce repetition and improve generation quality on open-ended and instruction-following tasks
- ⚠️ No results on standard benchmarks (MMLU, GSM8K, etc.); tested model scales not disclosed

#### Summary
MoE models typically fix the number of active experts per layer regardless of task difficulty or layer role, leaving redundancy unaddressed. MetaNet is a support-set controller that predicts, per layer, an expert-retention threshold and a bounded routing bias, while keeping the backbone, experts, and router fully frozen. On DeepSeek-MoE-16B-Chat, a conservative setting activates 3.61 experts on average (40% fewer than fixed k=6) with comparable MMLU accuracy (0.489 vs. 0.474), and an aggressive setting reduces activations by 62% with only ~3.7 percentage-point accuracy drop. A controller trained on MMLU transfers to C-Eval without retraining, activating 2.90 experts on average (52% fewer) at 0.386 accuracy.

- 🎯 MetaNet predicts per-layer expert-retention thresholds and routing biases from a support set, with backbone/experts/router frozen

- 📊 Aggressive setting: 2.28 average active experts (−62%), MMLU accuracy ~3.7 pp lower than baseline
- 💡 MMLU-trained controller transfers zero-shot to C-Eval: 2.90 active experts on average (−52%), accuracy 0.386
