#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: a 35B model costs 19.5GB at INT4, and naive SSD offloading fails because next-layer expert selection cannot begin until the current layer's output is available. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer lightweight head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead—and uses that prediction directly as the routing decision, enabling SSD reads to overlap with compute and eliminating stalls. A recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates for quality loss from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks; the framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open-sourced.

- 🎯 Prerouter predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead, enabling SSD prefetch to overlap with compute and eliminating offloading stalls

#### Summary
Ternary LLMs led by BitNet b1.58 lack efficient edge inference support. Bitnet.cpp addresses this by targeting mixed-precision matrix multiplication (mpGEMM), the dominant cost in ternary LLM inference, with a dedicated library featuring two solutions: Ternary Lookup Table (TL), which fixes spatial inefficiencies of bit-wise methods, and Int2 with a Scale (I2_S), which guarantees lossless inference. The system achieves up to 6.25x speedup over full-precision baselines and up to 2.32x over low-bit baselines, with no accuracy loss. An extension to element-wise lookup tables (ELUT) for broader low-bit LLMs is also presented.

- 🎯 TL and I2_S methods enable sub-2-bits-per-weight lossless mpGEMM for ternary LLMs on edge devices

#### Summary
LLM deployment is constrained by memory and compute costs, and existing quantization methods struggle to maintain fidelity below 1 bit per weight. LittleBit decomposes weights via low-rank latent matrix factorization and binarizes the resulting factors, then applies a multi-scale compensation mechanism across row, column, and latent dimensions to recover lost information. Training is enabled by two contributions: Dual-SVID decomposition for QAT initialization and Residual Compensation to reduce approximation error. At 0.1 BPW, LittleBit compresses Llama2-13B to under 0.9 GB (~31× memory reduction) and outperforms leading methods operating at 0.7 BPW on Llama2-7B, with an estimated 11.6× inference speedup over FP16.
