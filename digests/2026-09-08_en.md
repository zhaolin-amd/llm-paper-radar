#### Summary
Floating-point quantization introduces a multiplicative noise profile that makes quantization error data-dependent, complicating theoretical analysis. This work develops a second-order theory of quantization noise in matrix multiplication, reducing data dependence to a scalar participation factor κ and deriving a closed-form SNR law. It further proves a closed-form upper bound κ* that no function-preserving linear transform can surpass, and shows a recent SOTA method already attains it. Building on this theory, KBBQ (Kappa-Braked Blockwise Quantization) is introduced, parameterizing how closely a transform approaches κ*, and outperforms prior SOTA at W4A4 across four base models and two FP4 formats without additional deployment-time computation.

#### Summary
Existing dynamic layer routing methods treat each routing decision as a local operation conditioned solely on the current hidden state, ignoring the sequential, path-dependent nature of routing across model depth. HeRo introduces a router memory mechanism built via linear attention that incrementally aggregates preceding routing scores and residual updates into a compact history representation, allowing each router to jointly condition on accumulated history and the current hidden state. Training only lightweight routers and adapters on a frozen backbone, HeRo achieves 100.24% of dense model performance across seven benchmarks while bypassing 26.87% of Llama 3.1-8B parameters, and retains 97.01% while bypassing 38.82%, consistently outperforming ten baselines.
