Many-shot ICL places severe KV cache memory pressure on LLM inference due to linear scaling with the number of demonstrations, bottlenecking both online serving and on-device deployment. MILO addresses this with a block-wise low-rank compression framework that factorizes the KV cache at block granularity, where each block spans multiple many-shot examples. Rank budgets are dynamically allocated per block using information entropy, aggressively compressing redundant blocks while preserving high-fidelity representations for critical ones. On Qwen2.5 models, MILO achieves up to 50% KV cache memory reduction and 1.8x throughput improvement with negligible degradation on classification and reasoning benchmarks.

#### Summary
Prefix caching is essential for reducing prefill cost in long-running LLM applications, yet eviction behavior under agentic workloads is not well understood. Using production traces from two companies, this work evaluates 14 eviction algorithms under both HBM-constrained and large memory-pool settings, finding that sophisticated policies offer little benefit over LRU because the regular pacing of active sessions makes recency highly predictive. The paper introduces the compute-savings ratio and two offline oracles to quantify miss costs, which grow with sequence length due to increasing attention computation. Effective prefix-cache management is shown to require LRU as a foundation, augmented by quick demotion for one-hit prefixes, compute-aware partial eviction, and capacity-dependent eviction granularity.
