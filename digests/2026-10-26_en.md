#### Summary
Serving MoE models on consumer hardware is complicated by the fact that layer N+1's expert selection must be known before layer N's output is available, preventing naive SSD offloading from hiding I/O latency behind compute. Edge0 introduces a per-layer prerouter head that predicts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead and uses that prediction as the actual routing decision, enabling full compute–prefetch pipelining with no dropped experts. Quality degradation from int4 quantization and routing replacement is recovered by an unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path. On a single 24GB machine, the system serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Per-layer prerouter head predicts next-layer expert routing 1 token ahead, fully hiding SSD prefetch behind compute

- 💡 Unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates for int4 quantization and routing-replacement quality loss
- 🔧 Framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open source; 8B tier supported on the same framework

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**KV cache compression** · topic 4/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 8/10 · BellowsKV proposes a training-free cross-layer KV cache compression scheme: it maps the hidden states of n adjacent layers to a single shared latent, from which each layer reads its own KV via a layer-specific projection; the latent is kept at full dimension and then either quantized (BellowsKV-Quant) or subject to shared eviction (BellowsKV-Evict). Validated on Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, and Qwen2.5-7B, the method retains 97-98% accuracy at 13-14x compression, significantly outperforming existing cross-layer methods. The approach is simple (training-free, no calibration required) with a clear theoretical motivation and strong deployment feasibility. Limitations: the largest tested model is only 7-8B, and accuracy is reported as percentage of full-cache accuracy rather than named benchmarks (e.g., MMLU/GSM8K), reducing topic_relevance to 4 rather than 5; end-to-end latency speedup data is also absent. Overall categorized under kv_cache with practicality rated 4.

#### Summary
KV cache memory grows linearly with the number of layers and becomes the main GPU serving bottleneck for LLMs. Existing single-layer compression (quantization, channel reduction, token eviction) and cross-layer sharing methods both degrade sharply at high compression ratios. BellowsKV observes that adjacent layers share highly similar input hidden states, and maps the hidden states of n adjacent layers to a single shared latent per token; each layer then reconstructs its own keys and values via a layer-specific projection. The latent is kept at full dimension and compressed either by low-precision quantization (BellowsKV-Quant, nearly lossless due to the absence of dominant outlier channels) or by shared token eviction decisions (BellowsKV-Evict). On Llama-3.1-8B, Mistral-7B, and Qwen2.5-7B, the method retains ~99% of full-cache accuracy at 3.6x compression and 97-98% at 13-14x, versus at most 84% for the strongest cross-layer baseline at 16x.

- 🎯 Maps hidden states of n adjacent layers to one shared full-dimension latent; each layer reads its KV via a layer-specific projection, training-free with no calibration needed
- 💡 Adjacent-layer KV representations differ substantially while hidden states are highly similar; compressing latent dimension hurts far more than quantizing it
- 📊 Retains ~99% full-cache accuracy at 3.6x and 97-98% at 13-14x compression; best cross-layer baseline achieves only 84% at 16x
- ⚠️ Evaluated only on 7-8B models; no end-to-end latency or throughput data reported

#### 📎 Related methods / baselines

This paper implements end-to-end MXFP8 RL in the verl framework (Megatron/Transformer Engine training with vLLM and SGLang rollout) and measures behavior on B200 GPUs using Qwen3-8B and Qwen3-30B-A3B across dense and MoE configurations.
