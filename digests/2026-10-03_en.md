#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: at 4-bit the weights occupy 19.5GB, and MoE sparsity reduces compute per token but not the bytes that must be fetched, making naive SSD offloading ineffective because routing decisions for layer N+1 cannot be known until layer N finishes. Edge0 introduces a per-layer prerouter that predicts the next layer's expert selection one token ahead, allowing SSD reads to be issued in parallel with the current layer's compute with no expert dropping. A recovery LoRA trained on the student execution path compensates for quality loss from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 achieves 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

#### Summary
In codebook-based LLM weight quantization, imbalanced nearest-codeword assignments leave some codewords insufficiently updated, reducing effective codebook utilization. BARQ computes joint soft assignments between weight blocks and codewords via entropically regularized optimal transport with uniform marginals and curvature-weighted reconstruction costs, guaranteeing equal positive fitting mass for every codeword. Codewords are then refined through an assignment-weighted barycentric update, which is proven to minimize the fitting objective for fixed assignments; the soft assignments are subsequently discarded and the refined codebook is used for standard hard encoding. Across multiple LLMs, BARQ achieves lower perplexity and higher mean zero-shot accuracy than evaluated baselines at comparable bit budgets.

- 🎯 Entropically regularized optimal transport with uniform marginals ensures balanced codeword assignments during codebook fitting
- 💡 Assignment-weighted barycentric update is theoretically proven to minimize the fitting objective under fixed assignments
- 📊 Outperforms baselines in both perplexity and mean zero-shot accuracy across multiple LLMs at comparable bit budgets
- ⚠️ No end-to-end inference speedup reported; maximum tested model scale not specified

#### 🧭 Why this paper

Key findings include: WikiText-2 PPL cannot predict semantic loss (nearly identical PPL penalties correspond to definition loss gaps of 3.6% vs. 43.6%); parameter count dominates loss (Spearman rho=0.72) while tokenizer vocabulary size has no predictive power (rho=0.12); Q4_K_M preserves semantics cleanly at ≥1B; and sub-2B models exhibit severe semantic dissociation at Q2_K. #### Summary
GGUF mixed-precision K-quant quantization is the dominant path for deploying open-weight models on consumer hardware, but its effect on fine-grained lexical semantics has not been systematically characterized. The study audits 27 quantized artifacts across 13 families and four architecture backbones (0.35B–14B parameters), evaluating down to Q2_K (~2.6 bits per weight) on 429 frequency-validated rare English words under surface-inclusion and one-sentence definition probes. In sub-2B models at Q2, definition scores fall 20–67% below artifact baselines — typically several times the surface inclusion loss — while WikiText-2 perplexity fails entirely as a predictor: near-identical PPL penalties (44.7% vs. 47.6%) correspond to definition losses of 3.6% vs. 43.6%. Parameter count dominates damage (Spearman rho=0.72); tokenizer vocabulary size has no predictive power (rho=0.12); and Q4_K_M remains lexically clean at ≥1B parameters.

- 🎯 Method: 27 GGUF artifacts across 13 families (0.35B–14B), evaluated down published quantization ladders to Q2_K (~2.6 bpw) on 429 rare words with inclusion and definition probes.
- 📊 Result: Sub-2B models at Q2_K show 20–67% definition score drops below baseline; Q4_K_M preserves lexical semantics cleanly at ≥1B parameters.
- 💡 Novelty: WikiText-2 PPL is uncorrelated with semantic damage — near-identical PPL penalties (44.7%/47.6%) yield definition losses of 3.6% vs. 43.6%; parameter count dominates (rho=0.72), vocabulary size does not (rho=0.12).
- ⚠️ Limitation: Aggressively quantized small models remain fluent while losing semantic knowledge; per-artifact validation is required since PPL is insufficient as a deployment quality signal.
