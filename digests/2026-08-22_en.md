#### Summary
Autoregressive LLMs suffer from sequential decoding, causing high inference latency and poor GPU utilization; existing speculative decoding methods still rely on autoregressive drafting and thus remain limited in practical speedup. DFlash introduces a lightweight block diffusion model as the drafter, generating multiple draft tokens in a single forward pass while conditioning on context features extracted from the target LLM to improve acceptance rates and output quality. Experiments across a range of models and tasks demonstrate over 6x lossless acceleration, with up to 2.5x higher speedup than the state-of-the-art method EAGLE-3.

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Survey / methodology & comparison** · topic 5/5 · practicality 5/5 · composite 10/10 · MoEXBench is a systematic benchmark study targeting composable compression of MoE LLMs (expert pruning + weight quantization + KV-cache compression) with end-to-end evaluation covering 10 MoE models at 30B–235B scale, assessing 20%–50% expert pruning, 1–16 bit quantization, and multiple KV-cache precision settings both individually and in combination. #### Summary
MoE LLMs face three deployment challenges on commodity hardware: large expert parameter footprints, routing imbalance, and KV-cache growth under long contexts, which in practice require stacking expert pruning, weight quantization, and KV-cache compression simultaneously—yet their interactions have never been systematically studied. MoEXBench evaluates 10 MoE models from 30B to 235B parameters across standard-attention, hybrid linear-attention, and sliding window attention architectures, testing 20%–50% expert pruning rates, 1–16 bit quantization schemes, and multiple KV-cache precision settings both individually and in combination via an eight-module evaluation suite. Key findings include: composable compression effects cannot be predicted from standalone techniques, expert pruning is the dominant source of quality degradation, compression rate alone does not reliably predict quality loss or runtime gains, and average quality metrics can hide workload- and architecture-specific failures. MoEXBench releases normalized module scores, compressed artifacts, and reproducible scripts to enable practical accuracy–memory–latency comparisons across MoE families and hardware backends.
