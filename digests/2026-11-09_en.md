#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware is memory-bound: at 4-bit quantization the weights reach 19.5GB, and naive SSD offloading fails because expert selection for layer N+1 requires layer N's output, preventing early reads. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead—and uses that prediction directly as the routing decision, allowing SSD prefetches to fully overlap with compute. A recovery LoRA trained on the student execution path compensates for quality lost to both int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Prerouter predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead; prediction is used directly as routing, fully hiding SSD reads behind compute

- 💡 Unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path jointly recovers quality lost to int4 quantization and routing replacement
- 🔧 Framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open-source; supports both 35B and 8B MoE tiers
