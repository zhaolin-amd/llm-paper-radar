#### Summary
Lattice-based vector quantization (VQ) offers strong rate-distortion trade-offs for low-bit LLM compression, but VQ quantization-aware training (QAT) at scale remains poorly understood. SQuAT decomposes weights into direction and magnitude: normalized directions are quantized with an E8-derived spherical codebook, while gains are quantized independently using low-bit scalar quantizers, with separate straight-through estimators (STEs) for each component, implicitly inducing Riemannian spherical optimization via tangent-space gradient updates. #### 🌊 Paper River

#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is memory-bound: a 35B-class model occupies 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity reduces compute per token but not the weight bytes that must be fetched. Naive SSD offloading fails because layer N+1's expert selection depends on layer N's output, preventing early prefetch. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a lightweight per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead and uses that prediction directly as the routing decision, fully hiding SSD reads behind compute. An unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path (int4 + predicted routing) compensates for both quantization and routing-replacement quality loss. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 delivers 20tok/s for a 35B MoE within 3GiB peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Prerouter predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead, enabling SSD reads to fully overlap with compute
- 💡 Recovery LoRA trained on the student path jointly compensates for int4 quantization and routing-prediction errors
- 📊 35B MoE runs at 20tok/s on a single 24GB machine with only 3GiB peak active memory, near fp16 quality on 5 benchmarks
- 🔧 Framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open-sourced; supports both 35B and 8B MoE tiers

#### Summary
Diffusion Language Models (DLMs) face unique quantization challenges in their iterative denoising process: quantization sensitivity is both task-dependent and timestep-dependent, with different downstream tasks requiring different precision levels at different denoising steps. T²Quant leverages a label-free sensitivity signal—requiring no ground-truth labels or task-specific metrics—to optimally allocate activation precision across denoising steps under a fixed bit-width budget. Experiments across three DLMs and six benchmarks show T²Quant consistently improves average accuracy of existing quantization methods by up to 6.7 percentage points under the same budget.
