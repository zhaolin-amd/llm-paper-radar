#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: a 35B-class model occupies 19.5GB at 4-bit, and naive SSD offloading fails because layer N+1 expert selection cannot begin until layer N produces its output, leaving I/O latency fully exposed. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead—and uses that prediction directly as the routing decision, aligning SSD prefetch windows with compute and eliminating stalls. A recovery LoRA trained on the student path (int4 + prerouter-replaced routing) compensates for quality loss. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB peak active memory, staying within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Prerouter predicts next-layer routing one token ahead; prediction is used as the actual route, fully overlapping SSD reads with compute
