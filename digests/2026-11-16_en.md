On Llama3 and Qwen3 at approximately 1.25 effective bits per element, MiSTiQ achieves lower WikiText perplexity and higher GSM8K accuracy than all evaluated codebook-based baselines, and with NVFP4 achieves the highest LongBench average on both models.

- 🎯 Trellis quantization + beam search encodes KV cache over large reconstruction spaces; MX-Hash functions generate reconstruction values directly in target MX format
- 📊 At ~1.25 effective bits/element, surpasses all codebook-based baselines on WikiText PPL and GSM8K accuracy for Llama3 and Qwen3
- 📊 With NVFP4, achieves highest LongBench average on both Llama3 and Qwen3
- ⚠️ Beam search encoding runtime overhead not quantified; no end-to-end speedup reported; benchmark coverage is limited

#### 📎 Related methods / baselines
- codebook-based vector quantization — replaced by MiSTiQ; hardware-constrained codebook size limits its scalability
- NVFP4 — used as target MX format; MiSTiQ achieves highest LongBench score under this format

👥 Anik Jha (Independent Researcher) · 🏷 cs.LG
🔗 [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20419) · [alphaxiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2611.20419)
📡 Sources: arxiv
🧪 pruning_distill · MoE expert pruning + rank-8 LoRA adapter recovery + 2-bit quantization · 122B MoE reasoning model · cal: Layer-wise LoRA fitting, rank-8, using only the kept experts' activations; cost not explicitly quantified. · perf: Model compressed to a 12 GiB file; Method B inference wall-time is 1.7× that of the baseline.

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Pruning / distillation** · topic 4/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 8/10 · This paper studies accuracy recovery after 75% expert pruning combined with 2-bit quantization on a 122B-parameter MoE reasoning model. Key contributions include: (1) identifying a specific failure mode caused by pruning (looping reasoning), and (2) proposing a layer-wise rank-8 LoRA adapter approach (Method B) that requires no changes to quantized weights. A pre-registered LiveCodeBench evaluation shows a significant improvement (+9.2 pp) with statistical credibility. The method is practically deployable: the adapter is loaded without re-quantization, the 12 GiB target file size is highly practical, and the layer-wise fitting pipeline is relatively straightforward. Limitations include validation on only one model and one benchmark family, no meaningful gain on HumanEval+/MBPP+, and a real 1.7× inference overhead. Overall, this is a practical combination of MoE expert pruning and low-rank recovery worth attention.

#### Summary
Pruning 75% of routed experts from a 122B-parameter reasoning MoE model reduces it to a 12 GiB file, but causes a specific failure mode on LiveCodeBench: verbatim reasoning loops on 44 of 120 development problems, versus none for the unpruned model at the same 2-bit quantization. Without modifying the kept experts' quantized bytes, sampling penalties and a stop-and-redraw policy recover part of the loss. Method B adds rank-8 LoRA adapters to the kept experts and restores two experts per layer, fitted layer-by-layer to the unpruned model's expert block outputs using the pruned model's own activations. In a pre-registered test on 130 LiveCodeBench problems, B solves 116 vs. 104 (+9.2 pp, 95% CI [+3.1, +15.4]), while showing no meaningful gain on HumanEval+ (+0.6 pp) or MBPP+ (-1.9 pp).

- 🎯 Method B: rank-8 LoRA adapter on kept experts, layer-by-layer fitting to unpruned activations, restoring 2 experts per layer without touching quantized weights
- 📊 Pre-registered LiveCodeBench: 116 vs. 104 problems solved (+9.2 pp, 95% CI [+3.1, +15.4]); 15 gained, 3 lost
- 📊 Model compressed to 12 GiB; B incurs 1.7× wall-time cost; HumanEval+ +0.6 pp, MBPP+ −1.9 pp (both within 2 pp tolerance)
- ⚠️ Evidence from one model, one quantization (2-bit), and one benchmark family; generalization not established
