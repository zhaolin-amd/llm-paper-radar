#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory—a 35B model still occupies 19.5GB at int4—and naive SSD offloading fails because expert reads cannot begin until after the routing decision from the previous layer. Edge0 introduces a prerouter, a per-layer lightweight head that predicts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead, enabling SSD prefetch to fully overlap with computation with no dropped experts. A recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates for quality loss from both int4 quantization and routing substitution. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks; the framework, checkpoints, and adapters are fully open-sourced.

#### Summary
Ultra-low-bit LLM backbones (e.g., 1.58-bit ternary) reduce weight storage and linear-layer costs, while multi-token prediction (MTP) reduces autoregressive decoding steps, but their naive combination fails to accelerate generation at comparable quality due to missing intermediate token inputs in masked drafting and overhead from wider computation and causal KV updates.
