#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is bottleneck by weight memory: a 35B model is 19.5GB at INT4, and sparse activation does not reduce the bytes that must be held. Naive SSD offloading fails because next-layer expert selection cannot begin until the current layer's output exists, leaving no time to hide I/O latency behind compute. Edge0 solves this with a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead—so SSD reads can be issued early and the predicted routing is used directly as the actual routing. A recovery LoRA trained on the student path (prerouter-replaced routing + INT4 quantization) jointly compensates for quality loss from both. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Prerouter predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead, enabling SSD prefetch fully overlapped with compute; prediction serves as the actual routing.

- 💡 Recovery LoRA trained on the student path simultaneously compensates for INT4 quantization error and routing-replacement quality loss.
