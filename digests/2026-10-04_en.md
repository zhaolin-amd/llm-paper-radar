#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: at 4-bit quantization the model still occupies 19.5GB, and naive SSD offloading cannot hide I/O latency because expert selection for layer N+1 depends on layer N's output. Edge0 introduces a per-layer prerouter head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead, enabling SSD prefetch to fully overlap with compute; an unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path then compensates quality loss from both int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 delivers 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Survey / methodology & comparison** · topic 4/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 8/10 · This paper systematically measures numerical divergence across GPU vendors (AMD vs NVIDIA) under identical model weights, covering dense and MoE models with five metric families (bitwise equality, logit differences, top-K consistency, token agreement, task accuracy / MMLU), and traces the error source to accumulation order in matrix instructions. The core findings are directly actionable for compression engineers: (1) FP32 upcasting is effective only for MLP layers (1.3× overhead vs 3× for full upcasting), providing concrete guidance for precision strategies in quantization calibration / inference frameworks; (2) FP16 fails in dense models and changes expert selection in MoE models, serving as a warning for cross-platform deployment of low-precision formats such as FP8 / W4A8 during calibration; (3) error propagates into distillation training, affecting KD recipe consistency. Code is open-sourced.

#### Summary
Running the same language model weights on different GPU vendors (AMD vs NVIDIA) produces divergent logits due to differing accumulation orders in matrix instructions. Five metric families — bitwise equality, logit differences, top-K consistency, token agreement, and task accuracy — are measured across two dense and two MoE models to characterize this cross-vendor mismatch. FP32 upcasting reduces dense-model logit error by 43% at 3× runtime, while restricting upcasting to MLP layers retains 94% of that gain at only 1.3× runtime; FP16 fails in dense models and alters expert selection in MoE models; a LoRA adapter on the output head provides no benefit. The mismatch propagates into distillation training, causing students distilled from AMD- and NVIDIA-hosted teachers to disagree on 431 MMLU questions.

- 📊 MLP-only FP32 upcasting retains 94% of full-upcasting gain at 1.3× runtime vs 3× for full FP32 upcasting
- ⚠️ FP16 fails in dense models; in MoE models FP32/FP16 reduce probability error but increase logit error and change expert selection
- 📊 Distillation mismatch: AMD- vs NVIDIA-distilled students differ on 431 MMLU questions with all seeds fixed
