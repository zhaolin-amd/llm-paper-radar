#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware (single 24GB GPU) is blocked by a memory wall: even at 4-bit quantization the model is 19.5GB, and naive SSD offloading fails because layer N+1's expert selection depends on layer N's output, preventing early prefetching. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer lightweight prediction head that predicts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead—so SSD reads are fully overlapped with compute and the staged expert set exactly matches the routed set with no drops. Combined with int4 quantization and an unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path to compensate both quantization loss and routing-replacement error, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory on a single 24GB machine, staying within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Prerouter: per-layer head predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead, fully overlapping SSD I/O with compute; staged and routed expert sets are identical
- 💡 Unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student inference path compensates both int4 quantization error and routing-replacement error jointly
- 📊 Single 24GB machine: 35B MoE at 20tok/s with 3GiB peak active memory, within a few points of fp16 teacher on five public benchmarks
- 🔧 Framework, checkpoints, and adapters are fully open-source; an 8B tier runs on the same framework

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Survey / methodology & comparison** · topic 5/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 9/10 · This paper conducts an in-depth measurement study of activation quantization, with the core finding that perplexity as an aggregate metric severely underestimates the damage activation quantization inflicts on specific capabilities such as induction and retrieval—highly valuable for guiding PTQ recipe selection. Coverage spans 12 models across four families, tested up to 32B parameters, and validated under real AWQ deployment configurations. Rotated-basis quantization substantially restores induction (0.001→0.980 at 3-bit) but recovers retrieval less effectively, giving compression engineers clear actionable guidance: PPL targets alone are insufficient to ensure model quality, and task-level diagnostic metrics are necessary. This is a high-value work within the survey bucket that produces new measurements and actionable conclusions, with high practicality since engineers can directly apply these findings to improve existing PTQ evaluation pipelines.

#### Summary
Perplexity is a reliable aggregate signal for activation quantization—across 12 models from four families and 780 within-model comparisons, the lower-perplexity arm retains more induction and retrieval in 97.9% and 96.0% of cases, respectively—but it systematically understates task-level damage: when perplexity rises by only 1.2–1.5×, retrieval accuracy has already collapsed to 0.554 while induction remains at 0.959. The damage is not explained by error magnitude: matched Gaussian noise of equal per-channel magnitude and sign-randomized quantization error are both largely harmless, pointing to coordinate alignment as the culprit. Quantizing in a rotated basis restores induction from 0.001 to 0.980 at three average bits per token in a single-block intervention (retrieval recovers less fully to 0.694); end-to-end at four average bits, induction reaches 0.968 and retrieval 0.534, with findings confirmed on models up to 32B parameters and under AWQ at 4-bit activations.

- 🎯 Method: 780 within-model comparisons across 12 models (4 families, up to 32B) separating perplexity from induction/retrieval task accuracy
- 📊 Result: At only 1.2–1.5× perplexity increase, retrieval drops to 0.554 while induction holds at 0.959—a gap perplexity does not surface
- 💡 Novelty: Rotating the quantization basis restores induction from 0.001 to 0.980 at 3-bit single-block intervention, showing damage stems from coordinate alignment, not error magnitude
- ⚠️ Limitation: Retrieval recovers poorly under the same rotated-basis setting (0.534 end-to-end at 4-bit); PPL targets alone are insufficient to guarantee task-level quality
