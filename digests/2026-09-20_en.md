#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is memory-bound rather than compute-bound: a 35B-class model occupies 19.5GB at 4-bit, and naive SSD offloading fails because the next layer's expert selection cannot be known early enough to overlap I/O with compute. Edge0 introduces a per-layer prerouter that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead and uses that prediction directly as the routing decision, enabling prefetching of expert weights from SSD. A recovery LoRA, trained unmerged on the student path, compensates quality loss from both int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Per-layer prerouter predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead, prediction used directly as routing to overlap SSD reads with compute
- 💡 Unmerged recovery LoRA trained on student path jointly corrects int4 quantization and routing-replacement quality loss

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**KV cache compression** · topic 5/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 9/10 · ValueDiff proposes a KV cache eviction method based on the L2 deviation of value vectors, targeting the failure of traditional attention sink signals in sink-suppressed LLMs (QK-norm, gated attention, etc.). The method is simple—eviction scores are L2 distances between value vectors and the cache mean—and is theoretically derived as minimal-disturbance eviction under a max-entropy assumption, requiring no calibration data. It demonstrates strong performance over existing baselines on three widely recognized benchmarks (RULER, LongBench, MATH-500), yielding high topic relevance. The algorithm is straightforward (a single L2 distance scoring step) and can be stacked on any KV eviction framework, giving good practicality, though the absence of end-to-end throughput/latency numbers and unclear maximum tested model scale slightly reduce the score.

#### Summary
Existing KV cache eviction methods rely on persistent attention sinks, which weaken substantially in LLMs with QK-normalization, gated attention, learned attention sinks, or logit softcapping. ValueDiff identifies that sink suppression co-occurs with greater value-vector dispersion relative to key-vector dispersion, and uses the L2 deviation of each token's value vector from the cache mean as the eviction score. This score is theoretically grounded as the minimal-disturbance eviction under a max-entropy assumption about future attention, requiring no calibration data. On RULER at a 2k token budget, ValueDiff retains 88–99% of dense performance across seven sink-suppressed models, ranking first on 6 of 7; on LongBench at 4k budget it averages 92% retention vs. 83% for the strongest prior baseline; on MATH-500 at 25% cache budget it outperforms prior methods by up to ~20 points on gated-attention models.

- 🎯 Ranks tokens by L2 deviation of value vectors from cache mean, equivalent to minimal-disturbance eviction under max-entropy future-attention assumption
- 📊 RULER 2k budget: 88–99% dense retention across 7 sink-suppressed models, best on 6 of 7
- 📊 LongBench 4k budget: 92% average retention vs. 83% for strongest prior baseline; MATH-500 25% budget: up to ~20-point gain on gated-attention models
- 💡 Data-free; score requires only an L2 distance computation, composable with any KV eviction framework
