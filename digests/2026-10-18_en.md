#### Summary
Existing sparse attention methods fail to bound per-query error and exhibit heavy-tailed errors on real KV caches. The proposed method perturbs each key's attention score with an independent Exp(1) variable, converting threshold retrieval into sampling proportional to attention weights; a geometric threshold grid yields nested samples, Horvitz–Thompson output estimates at every level, and simultaneous confidence bounds, so each head stops at the first level whose certificate meets the target error ε. The estimator reads O(ε⁻²log(1/δ)) values independent of context length, which is shown to be optimal up to constants. Across three LLMs and ten settings at 16K–128K tokens, no certificate is violated; at equal keys read, the 90th-percentile error is 4.6–7.9× lower than the best of 15 baseline families, and CPU decoding runs 1.1–1.4× faster than dense attention.

- 📊 CPU decoding 1.1–1.4× faster than dense attention at 32K–128K tokens; within 1 point on downstream tasks and 0.1% PG-19 perplexity vs. dense attention
- ⚠️ 4-bit deterministic scan for candidate generation still reads every key; GPU speedup path not demonstrated

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Pruning / distillation** · topic 3/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 7/10 · GCD falls under the self-distillation/knowledge distillation category: it feeds the gold response as context to the teacher side and trains the student on soft targets instead of one-hot labels, using a single model with no extra components. #### Summary
Standard SFT treats gold responses as one-hot targets, suppressing valid alternative continuations that the model would naturally assign probability to. Gold-Conditioned Distillation (GCD) uses a single model as both teacher and student: the teacher conditions on a prompt augmented with the gold response in context, while the student conditions on the original prompt as in inference, and is trained to minimize forward KL divergence to the teacher's next-token distribution at each gold token position. This allows the model to leverage its own prior knowledge to teach itself, anchoring correctness via the gold response while preserving plausible alternatives through soft targets. Across multiple training tasks on Qwen and Llama models, GCD improves new-task accuracy over SFT and generally reduces degradation on general benchmarks.

- 🎯 Single model serves as both teacher and student: teacher conditions on gold response in context; student aligns to teacher's soft distribution via forward KL at inference-matching prompts
- 💡 Soft targets anchor correctness to the gold response while preserving plausible alternative continuations, mitigating information loss from one-hot supervision
- 📊 Evaluated on Qwen and Llama across multiple tasks: GCD outperforms SFT on new-task accuracy and generally reduces degradation on general benchmarks
- 🔧 No additional model required; training cost is comparable to standard SFT and integrates directly into existing SFT pipelines
