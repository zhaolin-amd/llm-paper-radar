#### Summary
KV cache compression is typically evaluated by final-answer accuracy, which implicitly assumes that correct answers imply preserved reasoning support. This paper challenges that assumption using a controlled fixed-trace replay protocol that holds reasoning content fixed and measures whether compression retains usable information from an existing trace. Ten token-eviction KV compression methods and one quantization method are evaluated on three models across mathematical reasoning, scientific QA, clinical calculation, and long-context retrieval, using final accuracy, answer-chain consistency, and perturbation faithfulness as metrics. Token-eviction methods are shown to maintain competitive final-answer accuracy while substantially degrading chain support or perturbation faithfulness—a phenomenon termed the answer-evidence gap. Coverage-preserving quantization is substantially less affected, suggesting the gap is driven by loss of specific reasoning-trace segments rather than KV memory reduction per se.

- 🎯 Fixed-trace replay protocol isolates compression's effect on reasoning-chain usability, holding reasoning content constant
- 💡 Introduces the 'answer-evidence gap': token eviction preserves accuracy but degrades answer-chain consistency and perturbation faithfulness
- 📊 Benchmarks 10 token-eviction methods + 1 quantization method across 3 models and 4 task types
- ⚠️ Coverage-preserving quantization is substantially less affected, pointing to trace-segment loss—not memory reduction—as root cause
