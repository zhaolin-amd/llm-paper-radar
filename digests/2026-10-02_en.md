#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: at 4-bit quantization the model is 19.5GB, and naive SSD offloading fails because expert selection for layer N+1 is unknown until layer N finishes, preventing early prefetching. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead—so SSD reads can be issued before they are needed and fully overlap with compute. A recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates quality loss from both int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

- 🎯 Per-layer prerouter predicts next-layer expert routing one token ahead, enabling SSD prefetch to fully hide behind compute
- 📊 35B MoE served at 20 tok/s on a single 24GB GPU with only 3GiB peak active memory, near fp16 teacher on five benchmarks
- 💡 Recovery LoRA trained on the student path jointly compensates int4 quantization loss and prerouter routing-replacement error
- 🔧 Framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open-sourced; supports both 35B and 8B MoE tiers
