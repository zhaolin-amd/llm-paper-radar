#### Summary

#### Summary
Serving a 35B MoE on a single 24GB consumer GPU is memory-bound: at 4-bit the model weighs 19.5GB, and naive SSD offloading fails because layer N+1's expert selection depends on layer N's output, preventing early prefetching. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead—and uses that prediction directly as the actual routing, so expert reads fully overlap with compute. An unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates for quality lost to both INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 achieves 20 tok/s with only 3GiB of peak active memory and stays within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks; the framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open-sourced.

**KV cache compression** · topic 4/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 8/10 · CVSA proposes a closed-loop sparse attention method that performs verify-then-correct inside the kernel using the softmax mass coverage signal: high-coverage sparse drafts are accepted directly, while low-coverage heads trigger local recovery—no sampling, no draft model, and no speculative decoding infrastructure required. The method is training-free with a simple algorithm and low deployment cost. #### Summary
Existing sparse attention methods operate open-loop: once a KV cache subset is selected, there is no signal indicating whether sufficient attention mass was preserved and no recovery path if it was not. CVSA exploits the exact algebraic equality between the softmax mass fraction on selected tokens (coverage) and the per-step output error to build a training-free, closed-loop verify-then-correct decode loop inside the kernel—high-coverage drafts are accepted directly, while only heads falling short trigger local recovery, with no sampling, no draft model, and no speculative-decoding infrastructure. On LongBench at 7B and 70B, CVSA is within 1.13 points of dense attention (statistically matching at 70B), loses 0.8 points on RULER, and achieves a measured median decode-step speedup of 6.21× at 128K context.

- 📊 Within 1.13 points of dense attention on LongBench; statistically tied at 70B; 0.8-point loss on RULER; median decode-step speedup 6.21× at 128K context
- 💡 Only heads with insufficient coverage trigger recovery, avoiding full KV cache access with no speculative-decoding infrastructure required
- 📊 Quality varies by only 0.14 points across the full threshold sweep, indicating robust deployment hyperparameters

#### Summary
1-bit KV cache compression suffers from high reconstruction error and precision loss on critical tokens. CALFA-KV jointly fits a key center, radial normalization parameters, and K/V codebooks offline to minimize the projected attention output error of a frozen model, while estimating channel-wise Fisher-type sensitivities from squared gradients of a reference next-token cross-entropy loss. At inference time, these sensitivities weight per-KV-state reconstruction errors, and the highest-scoring states are retained at FP16 precision under a per-layer, per-KV-head budget. With 56 FP16 anchors per layer and KV head, CALFA-KV (d8b8) outperforms all nominal 1-bit baselines on mean score across seven benchmarks for three models; CALFA-KV (d8b10) at 1.25 bits per channel narrows the gap to BF16 to 0.90–2.01 points on the same metric.
