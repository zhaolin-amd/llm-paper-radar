#### Summary
Structured pruning removes weight columns to reduce LLM size and inference cost, but induces output error that degrades accuracy. Existing training-free compensation methods apply either an additive bias or a single orthogonal rotation on the output side of the retained weight, leaving its input singular frame unchanged and limiting post-pruning adaptation. COEC applies alternating left and right orthogonal rotations to the retained weight: the right rotation is optimized on a reduced Stiefel manifold, singular values are rescaled via generalized cross-validation (GCV) per layer, a Gram matrix tempering step suppresses high-energy activation directions, and an alignment penalty preserves the geometric relationship between adjacent attention projections. Using only second-order statistics from a small calibration set—no backpropagation or retraining—COEC improves perplexity on every tested model and zero-shot accuracy in most settings across Llama-3, Llama-3.1, and Qwen2.5 families at multiple sparsity levels, with larger gains at higher sparsity.

- 🔧 Requires only second-order statistics from a small calibration set; no backpropagation, no retraining, compatible with any column pruning criterion
