#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: at 4-bit quantization the weights occupy 19.5GB, and naive SSD offloading fails because the next layer's expert selection cannot be known early enough to prefetch ahead of compute. Edge0 addresses this with a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token in advance and uses that prediction directly as the routing decision, enabling full overlap of expert prefetch with compute. A recovery LoRA trained on the student execution path compensates for quality loss from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 achieves 20tok/s for a 35B MoE within 3GiB peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

#### Summary

Bitnet.cpp addresses this by providing an inference system built around a novel mpGEMM library that achieves sub-2-bits-per-weight, lossless inference through two kernels: Ternary Lookup Table (TL), which resolves spatial inefficiencies of prior bitwise methods, and Int2 with Scale (I2_S), which guarantees lossless inference on edge devices. #### Summary
LLM deployment is constrained by memory and compute requirements, and existing quantization methods struggle to preserve model fidelity below 1 bit per weight. LittleBit combines low-rank latent matrix factorization with binarization of the resulting factors, augmented by a multi-scale compensation mechanism that learns importance parameters across row, column, and latent dimensions. Effective QAT training is enabled by Dual Sign-Value-Independent Decomposition (Dual-SVID) for initialization and Residual Compensation to reduce approximation error. At 0.1 BPW, LittleBit on Llama2-7B outperforms leading methods operating at 0.7 BPW, compresses Llama2-13B to under 0.9 GB (~31× memory reduction), and unlocks a potential 11.6× inference speedup over FP16.

- 💡 Dual-SVID initialization and Residual Compensation jointly support stable QAT at extreme bit-widths
- ⚠️ 11.6× FP16 speedup is theoretical; no end-to-end kernel benchmarks reported
