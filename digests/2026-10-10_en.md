#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: a 35B-class model occupies 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity reduces per-token compute without reducing the bytes that must be stored. Naive SSD offloading fails because layer N+1's expert selection depends on layer N's output, preventing reads from starting early enough to overlap with compute. Edge0 addresses this with a prerouter: a per-layer prediction head forecasts the next layer's routing one token ahead, and its prediction is used directly as the routing decision, so the staged expert set matches the routed set exactly. A recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates for quality loss from both int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 runs a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.
