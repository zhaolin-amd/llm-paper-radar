#### Summary
In reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR), standard mixed-precision BF16 training converges slower than FP16 at the same low learning rate due to weight sticking: round-to-nearest quantization of FP32 shadow weights to BF16 often leaves the BF16 weights unchanged under small gradient updates. Raising the BF16 learning rate alleviates sticking but increases KL divergence from the reference model. Applying stochastic rounding when casting FP32 shadow weights to BF16 matches FP16 convergence speed across models and datasets while reducing KL divergence compared to high-learning-rate BF16, and requires no loss scaling.

- ⚠️ Targeted at low-learning-rate RLVR settings; standard pretraining BF16 already converges comparably to FP16

#### Summary
Weight-activation quantization reduces memory and compute costs at inference, but existing methods typically require expensive optimization and architecture-specific cross-layer propagation of basis transforms. MACaQ is an MXFP4 W4A4 post-training quantization method that treats every linear layer independently, applying a per-channel scale and block-Hadamard transform online to activations while absorbing inverse transforms into weights, enabling a streaming implementation that keeps only one layer in memory at a time. Flip-Refinement, a Hessian-aware coordinate-descent procedure, greedily shifts individual FP4 codes to adjacent representable values to improve over round-to-nearest. MACaQ adds only 2.3% end-to-end overhead over pure MXFP4 on Qwen3-32B in vLLM, achieves lower perplexity than DuQuant++ and MR-GPTQ, matches their best perplexity on Llama-3-8B 11× faster, and quantizes a 122B-parameter model in 64 minutes using only 32.5GB GPU RAM.

- 💡 Flip-Refinement: Hessian-aware coordinate descent that greedily moves individual FP4 codes to adjacent representable values
- 📊 Only 2.3% end-to-end overhead over pure MXFP4 on Qwen3-32B in vLLM; matches DuQuant++/MR-GPTQ perplexity on Llama-3-8B 11× faster
