#### Summary
Early community 4-bit quantizations of Qwen3.8-27B (48 Gated DeltaNet + 16 softmax attention layers) kept GDN blocks at 8- or 16-bit precision, fearing that recurrent error accumulates over long contexts. Minima applies NVFP4 W4A4 to all 496 linear layers, GDN gates included, and matches BF16 within seed noise across PPL (4K/32K), MMLU-Pro, GSM8K, AIME'25, GPQA-Diamond, LiveCodeBench, and RULER-64K (5-task average −0.52), while being the smallest (17.5 GiB) and fastest-prefill (+14–19%) recipe tested. The work also repairs a global-scale mismatch between per-module-calibrated NVFP4 checkpoints and fusion kernels, and demonstrates performance-free FP8 KV-cache scale calibration.

#### Summary
Long reasoning chains make the KV cache a severe memory bottleneck for LLMs, and all existing compression methods rely on scoring tokens by estimated future importance. Random Attention eliminates scoring entirely: it retains all prompt tokens and evicts uniformly at random within each attention head for the reasoning trace. Across four models and six reasoning tasks it matches the strongest score-based evictor while achieving 32–43% higher throughput in vLLM deployment. Controlled experiments show that (1) retaining the prompt is the decisive factor, and (2) reasoning traces self-protect via textual restatement and cross-head redundancy, so random eviction retains enough copies without any scoring.

#### Summary
Compressed context is typically stored as human-readable text or images that require decoding at inference time, even when the consumer is a language model. LatentPress converts conversational histories and long documents into continuous memory tokens that a frozen decoder reads directly via its input-embedding interface, eliminating text reconstruction at inference. Only a small adapter (4.2M–26.2M parameters, ~0.1% of the decoder) is trained, achieving 4–16× compression; on LongMemEval at 7.70× compression, it reaches 0.504 accuracy, surpassing uncompressed evidence (0.490), text summaries (0.184), and OCR-based compression (0.426–0.312).
