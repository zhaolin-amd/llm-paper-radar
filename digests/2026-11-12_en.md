#### Summary
MoE inference on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: a 35B-class model occupies 19.5GB at 4-bit, and sparsity reduces compute per token but not bytes loaded, making naive SSD offloading ineffective due to routing-dependent read latency. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead—so SSD prefetches can be fully overlapped with compute. A recovery LoRA trained on the student execution path compensates quality loss from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks; the full framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open-sourced.

#### Summary
Weight-activation quantization suffers from expensive optimization and architecture-specific cross-layer propagation of basis transforms. MACaQ addresses this with a layer-local MXFP4 W4A4 PTQ scheme: per-channel scaling and a block-Hadamard transform are applied online to each layer's activations, with inverse transforms absorbed into weights, entirely eliminating cross-layer dependencies. A Hessian-aware Flip-Refinement coordinate-descent procedure further refines FP4 codes by greedily moving them to adjacent representable values. MACaQ surpasses DuQuant++ and MR-GPTQ in perplexity while being 11× faster on Llama-3-8B, adds only 2.3% end-to-end overhead over pure MXFP4 in vLLM, and quantizes a 122B-parameter model in 64 minutes using 32.5GB GPU RAM.

- 📊 Beats DuQuant++ and MR-GPTQ in perplexity; matches their best on Llama-3-8B with 11× less quantization time
- 🔧 Quantizes a 122B model in 64 minutes with 32.5GB GPU RAM; only 2.3% end-to-end overhead over pure MXFP4 in vLLM
