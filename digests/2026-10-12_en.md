#### Summary
Layer-wise PTQ methods allow each layer to compensate for quantization error inherited from earlier layers; this paper analyzes under what conditions such compensation provably keeps the mean-square output error no larger than without it. - 🎯 Under linear networks with subtractive dither, fixed compensation strengths in [0,1] provably guarantee non-increasing mean-square output error, with an exact error-gain decomposition.
- 💡 A counterexample with two scalar linear layers and fixed rounding errors refutes a previously published universal compensation guarantee.
- ⚠️ Guarantees break under ReLU activations and bounded codebooks, due to state changes and error redistribution across active/inactive samples.
- 📊 On two 8B models at 4 bits, fixed half-strength compensation beats no compensation in all tests, yet no strength-selection rule consistently beats a layer-wise rule.
