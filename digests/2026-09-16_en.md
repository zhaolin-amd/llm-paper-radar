#### Summary
MoE language models face a severe memory bottleneck due to large parameter counts, and existing expert pruning methods treat each expert independently under a purely additive contribution assumption, ignoring cooperative interactions. HOPE (Higher-Order Pruning of Experts) derives a second-order pruning objective that provably minimizes an upper bound on pruning error, and shows that the state-of-the-art first-order method REAP is a special case of HOPE with interaction terms dropped. Evaluated on three frontier MoE models (up to 122B parameters) across math, coding, instruction following, and agentic benchmarks, HOPE achieves an average rank of 1.58 out of 5 methods at 50% pruning (versus 2.42 for REAP), with gains of up to +6.1% on agentic coding tasks.

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Survey / methodology & comparison** · topic 5/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 9/10 · This paper proposes a rigorous methodology for cross-system, cross-technique comparison of quality loss from inference optimizations. Its core contribution is a calibrated LLM-as-judge scoring pipeline (including null condition verification and noise quantification), making it a directly actionable empirical comparison work for compression deployment (survey bucket). Experiments cover 4-bit/3-bit quantization, early-exit, and speculative decoding, comparing domain sensitivity (English prose vs. Chinese vs. math) on Alibaba and Meta models. #### Summary
Existing LLM inference optimizations (quantization, early-exit, speculative decoding) each rely on idiosyncratic benchmarks, preventing cross-technique quality comparison. This work proposes a rigorous measurement methodology centered on a formally calibrated LLM-as-judge: the judge is validated by comparing two statistically equivalent runs of the same model, per-sample noise is quantified, and a provably distribution-identical null condition is included in every design. Applied uniformly across acceleration techniques, the framework finds that a 4-bit model is indistinguishable from its 16-bit original at ±0.3-point resolution on English and Chinese prose, while 3-bit precision costs 0.5 points on English prose, 0.9 on Chinese, and 1.1 on multi-step math; the same quantizer costs Meta's model 1.8 points versus only 0.7 for Alibaba's, revealing substantial inter-model sensitivity differences.

- 🎯 Calibrated LLM-as-judge framework with null condition verification and per-sample noise quantification enables cross-technique comparison
- 📊 4-bit quantization indistinguishable from 16-bit original at ±0.3-point resolution on English and Chinese prose
- 📊 Early exit costs 0.7 points on prose but 2.5 on math, dropping correctly solved problems from 19/27 to 6
- ⚠️ Token certainty predicts divergence from full-model choices but not whether that divergence affects judged quality
