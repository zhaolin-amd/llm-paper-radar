#### Summary
Deploying 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware is bottlenecked by weight memory: at 4-bit precision the model occupies 19.5GB, and naive SSD offloading fails because expert selection for layer N+1 cannot begin until layer N's output is available. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a lightweight per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token in advance—whose predictions are used directly as routing decisions, enabling SSD reads to be fully overlapped with compute without any expert dropping. An unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student execution path compensates for both INT4 quantization error and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, the system serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks; the framework, checkpoints, and adapters are open source.

#### Summary
Training binary-weight language models requires maintaining full-precision latent weights and Adam state, totaling ~97 bits per weight, which prevents on-device adaptation of deployed models. #### Summary
Large reasoning models incur two costs: large parameter count and long chain-of-thought (CoT) generation. Weight pruning and CoT compression address these separately but interfere: pruning introduces redundant reasoning on still-solvable problems and incomplete reasoning on newly unsolvable ones, reducing subsequent length-preference compressibility. CSD (Contrastive Set-Difference) identifies neurons enriched in redundant reasoning by contrasting their activations on long versus short correct solutions of the same problem, prunes them preferentially, and extends preference pairs to cover problems the pruned model always or never solves. Applied to DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B with 20% pruning, the combined approach uses 8–22% fewer thinking tokens across four math benchmarks compared to reasoning-aware calibration (RAC) pruning followed by the same compression, with accuracy within 0.5 points.

- 🎯 CSD contrasts neuron activations on long vs. short correct solutions of the same problem to identify and preferentially prune redundant-reasoning neurons
- 💡 Preference pairs extended to problems a pruned model always or never solves, closing the training-signal blind spot in CoT compression
- 📊 20% pruning of DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen-7B yields 8–22% fewer thinking tokens on four math benchmarks with ≤0.5-point accuracy drop
- ⚠️ Requires multi-stage pipeline (pruning + long-short solution pair collection + preference training); largest tested model is 7B; no end-to-end latency numbers reported
