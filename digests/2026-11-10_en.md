#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware is memory-bound: even at 4-bit the model weighs 19.5GB, and naive SSD offloading fails because expert selection for layer N+1 cannot begin until layer N's output is ready, leaving reads unable to hide behind compute. Edge0 introduces a prerouter—a per-layer head that predicts the next layer's routing one token ahead and whose prediction is consumed directly as the routing decision—so SSD prefetches fully overlap with computation. An unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates for quality lost to int4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20 tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of the fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

#### Summary
RidgeFill accelerates prefill by skipping computation of most upper transformer layers: lower layers run up to a split point, after which a ridge regression map—fitted once in closed form from a single pass over calibration text—predicts each upper layer's input and writes the KV cache directly, requiring no training and dropping no tokens. The approach retrofits models never trained for this purpose, such as Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3.8-27B. On Qwen3.8-27B with llama.cpp on an A100, time-to-first-token is 1.25–1.31× faster at 16K–120K tokens, with RULER dropping −1.2 to −1.4 points and LongBench v2 by −1.4 points. #### Summary
The nature of expert specialization in MoE models remains debated, with conflicting evidence between domain-level routing preferences and token/syntax-driven routing. This paper introduces Routing-Aware Magnitude Profiling (RAMP) to analyze domain-specialized computation at the neuron level, finding 58-74% neuron-set overlap across same-domain samples versus only 15-24% across domains. Tracking three fully open MoE families through pre- and post-training reveals that expert-level importance rankings are relatively stable during pre-training while neuron-level importance continues to reorganize between checkpoints, with post-training largely preserving neuron rankings. Building on these insights, the proposed RAMPr pruning method achieves 91-98% average performance retention across six models at 75% neuron retention, and at 50% retention reduces resident GPU memory by 36-43% and improves batched prefill throughput by 17-39%.
