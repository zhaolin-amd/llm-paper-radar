#### 🧭 Why this paper
**PTQ (post-training quantization)** · topic 4/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 8/10 · ExactMoE targets MoE model inference, applying W4A16 quantization only to routed experts stored in pinned host memory, achieving efficient inference via a GPU-resident slot cache and fused grouped MoE kernels. The approach is clean (MARLIN format + slot cache), requires no expert pruning or CPU-side execution, and achieves 87% memory reduction with 99.2% accuracy retention on a single L4 GPU. Evaluation is based on 12,450 zero-shot multiple-choice questions, lacking standard downstream benchmarks such as MMLU/GSM8K (limiting topic_relevance to 4). The deployment path is clear: PTQ calibration cost is low, kernels are integrated, and the method suits memory-constrained MoE deployment. The main limitation is that the largest tested model is OLMoE-1B-7B, with no validation on larger MoEs such as Mixtral-8x7B or Qwen3-MoE-235B.

#### Summary
MoE inference requires storing the full expert bank, imposing high memory overhead. ExactMoE applies symmetric group-128 W4 quantization exclusively to routed experts, stores them in MARLIN format in pinned host memory, and executes selected experts via a GPU-resident slot cache and fused grouped MoE kernels, while keeping the router, attention, embeddings, and LM head in BF16. On OLMoE-1B-7B on a single NVIDIA L4, a 16-slot configuration reduces peak GPU memory from 14.168 to 1.836 GiB (87.04%) while retaining 81.85% of BF16 decode throughput; a 64-slot configuration reaches 31.923 tokens/s versus 21.662 tokens/s for BF16, with 99.23% zero-shot accuracy retention across 12,450 questions.

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Pruning / distillation** · topic 5/5 · practicality 5/5 · composite 10/10 · MAPLE is a plug-and-play expert budget reallocation framework for MoE LLMs whose core contribution is a closed-form sensitivity-guided heterogeneous layer-wise expert allocation requiring no weight modification or retraining. Validated on four MoE models including DeepSeek-MoE-16B, it not only maintains accuracy under a 75% expert budget but surpasses the 100% uniform baseline on ARC-E/ARC-C/BoolQ, while achieving a measured 32.2% latency reduction and 47.4% throughput improvement in SGLang. The approach is concise (closed-form formula + genetic search), retraining-free, and already integrated into the production inference engine SGLang, making it a high-quality and practically deployable contribution to MoE compression.

#### Summary
Sparsely-activated MoE Transformers uniformly fix the same routed-expert count across all layers, ignoring well-documented layer-wise redundancy heterogeneity. MAPLE is a plug-and-play framework that reallocates the routed-expert budget heterogeneously across layers of any pretrained MoE LLM via a closed-form sensitivity-guided allocation, without weight modification or retraining; a sensitivity-constrained genetic search further refines the assignment. On four MoE models under a 75% routed-expert budget, MAPLE outperforms uniform and pruning-based baselines, and on DeepSeek-MoE-16B it surpasses the 100% uniform baseline on ARC-E (65.09→71.40), ARC-C (48.49→51.50), and BoolQ (80.03→82.38), while reducing SGLang single-GPU serving latency by 32.2% and improving throughput by 47.4%.

- 🎯 Closed-form sensitivity-guided heterogeneous expert allocation per layer, refined by a sensitivity-constrained genetic search; no retraining or weight changes required.
- 📊 DeepSeek-MoE-16B with only 75% experts exceeds 100% uniform baseline: ARC-E 65.09→71.40, ARC-C 48.49→51.50, BoolQ 80.03→82.38.
- 📊 SGLang deployment: single-GPU end-to-end serving latency reduced by 32.2%, throughput improved by 47.4%.
- 💡 Outperforms uniform and pruning-based baselines across four MoE models of varying scales and architectures under 75% expert budget.

#### 🧭 Why this paper
**Survey / methodology & comparison** · topic 5/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 9/10 · This is a high-quality methodology/measurement paper falling in the survey bucket: its main contributions are a preregistered audit of 17 model-compression equivalence claims and a paired equivalence testing statistical toolkit. The core findings are directly actionable for compression engineers: (1) churn (per-item disagreement rate) is ~5× the net accuracy delta, meaning net delta alone cannot establish model equivalence; (2) the relative ranking of GPTQ vs. AWQ flips across different calibration seeds, indicating that method rankings in existing comparison literature are unreliable; (3) a concrete five-line reporting standard is proposed (declare a margin, run the paired test, report churn alongside net delta, cite the sample size met, release per-item outputs). Model scales range from 1.3B to 405B, comparison methods are GPTQ and AWQ, squarely in the core LLM quantization setting. High practical utility: the statistical toolkit is open-sourced, calibration cost is low (reuses existing evaluation dumps), and it can be directly integrated into existing PTQ evaluation pipelines.

#### Summary
Existing equivalence claims for compressed models rely on net benchmark accuracy delta, which is least informative precisely when two models are most similar. Analysis of 1,707 paired model-by-task cells (1.3B–405B) shows churn runs roughly five times the net accuracy delta, and identically-scoring cells still disagree on individual items. A preregistered audit of 17 equivalence claims finds all 16 eligible claims state no prospective numerical margin and 5 provide insufficient information for numerical verification. The authors provide a paired equivalence testing toolkit with certification tables derived from observed compression disagreement rather than independent-binomial variance, and a controlled experiment shows that changing the calibration draw reversed the GPTQ-vs-AWQ ordering in 5 of 8 confirmatory cells across 5 seeds.

- 📊 Changing calibration draw reversed GPTQ vs. AWQ ordering in 5 of 8 confirmatory cells across 5 seeds
