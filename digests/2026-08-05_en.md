#### Summary
Query-agnostic KV cache eviction reuses a single compressed cache for all future queries, but performance collapses under tight budgets; existing work focuses on which KV pairs to retain. RestoreKV complements selection-based eviction with learned restoration under the same total KV budget: after prefill, a small set of restore tokens attends to the full KV cache in a single LoRA-adapted forward pass to produce a compact, context-conditioned restore cache, with adapters disabled for all subsequent queries and decoding. Training uses parameter-efficient self-distillation from a frozen full-cache model, updating only 0.4% of parameters with no task-specific tuning. Across four backbones and four long-context benchmarks, RestoreKV improves 59 of 60 budget-matched settings on Qwen3-4B; at a 5% budget, it raises KVzip from 38.2 to 73.2 on RULER-4K; combined with KVzip+, it achieves 86.4 RULER accuracy at 16× compression on the KVPress Benchmark, with under 0.5% one-time cache-construction overhead in a 32K-context evaluation.
