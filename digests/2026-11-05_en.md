#### 🧭 Why this paper
**KV cache compression** · topic 5/5 · practicality 4/5 · composite 9/10 · This paper proposes a learnable gate-based KV cache eviction method, validated on two standard long-context benchmarks—RULER and LongBench—with Qwen3-8B at 75% compression, showing drops of only 3.72 and 1.36 points respectively; results are credible. The core method designs the gate inside the softmax to ensure mass conservation, with thorough theoretical analysis. #### Summary
KV cache eviction requires identifying which cached entries carry information that the remaining cache cannot reconstruct. A learnable gate is placed inside the softmax normalizer so that suppressing one entry redistributes its attention mass to others rather than discarding it. Training this gate on the language-modeling loss with a frozen backbone reveals two fundamental obstacles: the dense-attention no-op is the gate's objective optimum (yielding near-constant scores), and gradients are contaminated by errors already present in the frozen model. - 💡 Evicted KV entries recovered via parameter-free centroids read inside the same softmax, outperforming any external memory mechanism

#### Summary
Long-context LLM attention inference suffers from both high computational cost and varying precision sensitivity across different tile interactions. TriAttention assigns query-key (QK) tiles to skip, 4-bit, or 8-bit computation, encoding each head's computation regions with two diagonal-distance boundaries so that policy storage is independent of context length. Offline calibration evaluates candidate boundary pairs using normalized projected-output errors and tile execution costs, then a model-wide allocator selects per-head boundaries to minimize calibration error under a shared compute budget.
