#### Summary
Efficiently serving coding agents is hindered by the lack of real workload data, as existing public traces and benchmarks do not reflect day-to-day multi-agent, multi-model usage. This work collects and releases a trace of ~4,300 coding-agent sessions comprising ~350,000 LLM steps and ~430,000 tool calls from real use of Claude Code and Codex. Analysis reveals that coding-agent workloads exhibit long autonomous loops, long contexts with short outputs, heavily-tailed tool-call distributions, and high but imperfect prefix cache hit rates. These findings motivate concrete serving optimizations: lower-overhead tool calling, append-length-aware prefill, semantic-aware tool-latency prediction, and improved KV-cache management around human-paced gaps.
