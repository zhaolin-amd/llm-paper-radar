#### Summary
Running 35B-class MoE models on consumer hardware faces two simultaneous bottlenecks: 19.5GB of INT4 weights exceed VRAM, while naive SSD offloading stalls because expert selection for layer N+1 cannot begin until layer N produces its output. Edge0 resolves this with a prerouter—a per-layer prediction head that forecasts the next layer's expert routing one token ahead—so prefetch I/O overlaps compute and the predicted expert set is used directly as the actual routing with no dropped or backup experts. An unmerged recovery LoRA trained on the student path compensates for quality degradation from both INT4 quantization and routing replacement. On a single 24GB machine, Edge0 serves a 35B MoE at 20tok/s within 3GiB of peak active memory, staying within a few points of its fp16 teacher across five public benchmarks.

#### Summary
Weight-activation quantization typically suffers from expensive cross-layer basis-transform propagation tied to specific architectures. MACaQ is an MXFP4 W4A4 post-training quantization method that treats every linear layer independently: it applies a per-channel scale and a block-Hadamard transform online to activations while absorbing the inverse transforms into weights, enabling a streaming implementation that keeps only one layer in memory at a time. Quantization quality is further improved by Flip-Refinement, a Hessian-aware coordinate-descent procedure that greedily moves individual FP4 codes to adjacent representable values. MACaQ reaches lower perplexity than DuQuant++ and MR-GPTQ, and matches their best perplexity on Llama-3-8B in 11× less quantization time; a 122B-parameter model can be quantized in 64 minutes using only 32.5GB GPU RAM, with only 2.3% end-to-end inference overhead over pure MXFP4 on Qwen3-32B in vLLM.

- 💡 Flip-Refinement: Hessian-aware coordinate descent greedily adjusts individual FP4 codes to adjacent representable values
- 📊 Matches DuQuant++ / MR-GPTQ best PPL on Llama-3-8B with 11× faster quantization
- 🔧 Quantizes a 122B model in 64 minutes with 32.5GB GPU RAM; adds only 2.3% end-to-end overhead on Qwen3-32B in vLLM

#### Summary
Post-training quantization damage in LLMs is unevenly distributed, yet intuitive hypotheses—that damage concentrates in task circuits, computation sites, or layers with extreme weight statistics—all fail to predict which layers benefit most from restored precision. Causal mixed-precision intervention (raising each layer to 8-bit in turn and measuring recovered accuracy) across 9 open-weight models in 4 architecture families shows that damage is diffuse: 8 of 9 models require roughly half the layers to recover 75% of the accuracy gap. At a matched precision budget, spending it globally on finer quantization granularity (group-128) beats locally repairing the most recoverable layers by 21–52 points on all 8 compatible models; 8-bit quantization is near-lossless under RTN, GPTQ, and AWQ, indicating the residual gap is budget-limited.

- 🎯 Causal per-layer 8-bit intervention across 9 models tests whether damage concentrates in circuits, compute sites, or weight-statistic outliers
- 📊 Global group-128 granularity outperforms local layer repair by 21–52 points on all 8 group-128-compatible models
- 💡 Quantization damage is diffuse in 8/9 models (≈half the layers needed for 75% gap recovery); Qwen3-8B is the lone concentrated exception
- 📊 8-bit is near-lossless across RTN, GPTQ, and AWQ, showing the accuracy gap is budget-limited, not method-limited
