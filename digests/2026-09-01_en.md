#### Summary
Standard forward KL distillation simultaneously improves reasoning and factual recall during pre-training, but during mid-training—an intermediate self-supervised phase on curated corpora—it diverges: reasoning keeps improving while factual recall acquisition is slowed. This stage dependence stems from an asymmetry in teacher confidence across data domains (higher on procedural, lower on knowledge-intensive data) and the student's earlier acquisition of low-entropy factual knowledge. Switch Distillation is proposed as a lightweight fix: using teacher predictive entropy as a token-level routing signal to apply KD where the teacher is confident and fall back to cross-entropy otherwise. #### Summary
PTQ accuracy loss is unevenly distributed across layers, yet common intuitions—task circuits, computation location, or weight statistics—fail to predict which layers benefit most from restored precision. Causal mixed-precision intervention (raising each layer to 8-bit and measuring accuracy recovery) across 9 open-weight models in 4 architecture families reveals that quantization damage is diffuse: recovering 75% of the accuracy gap requires roughly half the layers in 8 of 9 models. At a matched precision budget, globally applying finer quantization granularity (group-128) outperforms locally repairing the most recoverable layers by 21–52 points across all 8 group-128-compatible models, including the atypically concentrated Qwen3-8B.

- 🎯 Layer-wise causal intervention (each layer raised to 8-bit) serves as ground truth for measuring quantization damage across 9 models and 4 architecture families
- 📊 Global group-128 granularity outperforms local layer protection by 21–52 points at equal precision budget across all 8 compatible models
- 💡 Quantization damage is diffuse in 8/9 models; Qwen3-8B is the sole concentrated exception, yet global granularity still wins
- ⚠️ All evaluated models are ≤8B; OpenLLaMA excluded from global-vs-local comparison due to width incompatibility with group-128
