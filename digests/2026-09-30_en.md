#### Summary
Chunked KV-cache compression compresses windows of consecutive tokens into fewer cache entries at a fixed stride, introducing a new positional coordinate called 'phase'—a token's position relative to compression-window boundaries. This work systematically uncovers phase sensitivity: retrieval accuracy for the same information can differ by up to 40 percentage points across phases in large open-weight models, while average benchmark scores conceal these periodic weak spots. Through pretraining transformer families from scratch across multiple KV-compression designs and applying causal interventions, the study identifies phase specialization, where different attention components contribute asymmetrically to retrieval at different source phases. Gradient flow analysis on idealized retrieval models further explains why sharp phase specialization emerges during training, with the key practical implication that evaluation must cover all compression phases.
