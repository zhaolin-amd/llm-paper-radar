Overall scores: topic_relevance=4, practicality=3.

#### Summary
Standard knowledge distillation assumes the teacher is a reliable oracle, but in LLMs teacher predictions frequently exhibit high entropy and hallucinations, corrupting well-calibrated student priors. CaRE-KD addresses this with two components: CaRE-Divergence, a token-level loss that adaptively switches between Forward and Reverse KL based on teacher–student confidence, and Revival, a batch-level epistemic rejection mechanism that suppresses updates when the teacher is more uncertain than the student. Gradient-level analysis shows this dual-granularity design induces a conditional calibration effect that static divergences cannot reproduce. Across 8 teacher–student pairs and 11 benchmarks, CaRE-KD achieves up to +3.2 average ROUGE-L on instruction following, +2.1 pass@1 on MBPP, +1.7 on GSM8k, and +1.8 on CollegeMath over the strongest baseline.

- 📊 Over strongest baseline: instruction-following ROUGE-L +3.2, MBPP pass@1 +2.1, GSM8k +1.7, CollegeMath +1.8, LLM-as-judge factuality up to +2.5 per task over Skewed-RKL
- 💡 Gradient-level analysis proves the dual-granularity design yields a conditional calibration mechanism unreproducible by static divergences
- 🔧 Revival functions as a loss-agnostic plug-in that strengthens existing distillation objectives without architectural changes
