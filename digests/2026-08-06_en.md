#### Summary
GPTQ-style adaptive rounding for quantization typically relies on one-sided curvature information from input activations; two-sided Kronecker-factored Hessians can further capture output-coordinate correlations but naively applying GPTQ in the vectorized weight domain costs O(m²n²). BaKron introduces anti-diagonal parallelism combined with a recursive divide-and-conquer construction, reducing total work to O(mn(m+n)) with only O(m+n) sequential steps, matching GPTQ's cubic scaling while exploiting richer curvature. The solver is modular with respect to both the base quantizer and the Hessian estimator, and includes an efficient technique for Hessian computation, evaluated experimentally via reconstruction error and perplexity.
